Some UK bookmakers price NHL totals as if overtime doesn't count

Beat the Close · Published 13 October 2026

Our odds scanner kept flagging NHL Over 6.5 goals at Casumo, Grosvenor, LiveScore Bet and Virgin Bet as value against Pinnacle. It was always the over, never the under, and the gap never closed by puck drop. That pattern usually means the two books are pricing different bets, so we tested it.

The test

A game that's level after 60 minutes always has an even number of goals, and overtime adds exactly one. So on the 5.5 and 7.5 lines, "60 minutes" and "including overtime" settle the same. On 4.5, 6.5 and 8.5 they don't. If a book settles on 60 minutes, its price should match Pinnacle's on 5.5 and 7.5 and look generous on 6.5.

We compared each book's margin-free chance of the over with Pinnacle's, at the same moment, over about three weeks of NHL and European ice hockey. The figures are the average difference in percentage points, with the number of games in brackets.

The first five match Pinnacle where overtime can't matter, and sit 7 to 9 points lower where it can. That's about how often an NHL game is 3–3 after 60 minutes, the score that decides the 6.5 line. Coral, Ladbrokes and William Hill price like Pinnacle, so they appear to include overtime.

What it means

At those five books, an Over 6.5 that looks a few per cent "too big" is probably the correct price for a 60-minute bet. A game that's 3–3 after 60 minutes and decided in overtime wins at Pinnacle and loses there. Check your bookmaker's ice hockey rules before treating it as value.

The same trap elsewhere

Match odds in boxing, handball and rugby league are three-way at most UK books (a draw loses), while Pinnacle prices them two-way. Comparing the two makes the UK price look better than it is.

Caveats

This is inferred from prices, not from the bookmakers' rules pages, and it covers about three weeks of data.
